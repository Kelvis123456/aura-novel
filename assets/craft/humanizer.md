# humanizer
## When to use
Use when a passage reads as machine-written: inflated, promotional, formulaic, voiceless, or full of stock phrases. It removes those tells while keeping the meaning and the author's voice.

## Core rules
- Rewrite, do not delete. Cover everything the original covers, preserve the meaning, and match the intended register. If there is a writing sample, copy its sentence length, word level, punctuation habits, and transitions instead of upgrading them.
- Cut inflated significance: "stands as," "a testament to," "pivotal moment," "underscores," "reflects broader," "evolving landscape," "indelible mark." State the plain fact.
- Cut promotional language: "nestled," "vibrant," "rich," "breathtaking," "stunning," "boasts," "renowned." Use neutral, specific description.
- Cut superficial "-ing" tails (highlighting, ensuring, reflecting, fostering, showcasing) that add fake depth.
- Replace vague attributions ("experts argue," "observers have cited") with a specific source or remove them.
- Cut formulaic challenges-and-outlook endings and generic upbeat conclusions ("the future looks bright"). End on a concrete fact.
- Avoid AI vocabulary: delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, crucial, intricate, interplay, landscape (abstract), underscore, showcase, vibrant, enduring, foster, garner, additionally.
- Use plain "is/are/has" instead of "serves as," "features," "boasts," "represents."
- Remove negative parallelism ("not just X, it is Y," "not only... but") and clipped tailing negations ("no guessing").
- Break the rule of three when it is forced. Fix false ranges ("from X to Y" with no real scale) and elegant variation (cycling synonyms for one referent).
- Fix hidden actors and subjectless fragments when active voice is clearer.
- Hard rule: the final text contains no em dashes or en dashes, and no spaced double hyphens used the same way. Replace with a period, comma, colon, parentheses, or a restructured sentence. Scan before returning.
- Remove mechanical boldface, inline-header bullet lists, title-case headings, emojis, and curly quotes (use straight quotes).
- Remove chatbot artifacts ("I hope this helps," "Great question!"), signposting ("Let's dive in," "here is what you need to know"), fake-candid openers ("Honestly?," "Here is the thing"), and authority tropes ("the real question is," "at its core").
- Cut filler ("in order to" to "to," "due to the fact that" to "because") and excess hedging. Do not fill gaps with invented guesses: say what is unknown or cut it.
- Avoid manufactured punchlines and runs of short dramatic fragments, and aphorism formulas ("X is the language of Y"). One short emphatic sentence is fine.
- Add a human pulse only when the genre calls for it: vary rhythm, allow opinion, mixed feelings, specific unusual detail, asides, and a little mess. Do not flatten legitimate formal vocabulary, and do not rewrite phrases inside quotations or titles.
- Look for clusters of tells, not single ones. Preserve real human signs: hard-to-fabricate specifics, unresolved tension, mixed sentence lengths, genuine self-corrections.

## Diagnose
- Which sentences announce importance instead of stating a fact?
- Where is the language promotional rather than neutral?
- Which phrases appear in several places (stock words, threes, "not X but Y")?
- Is every sentence the same length and shape?
- Are there vague authorities, filler, or hedging?
- Does it open or close with ceremony (a signpost, a generic upbeat ending)?
- Are there em dashes, emojis, curly quotes, or decorative bold?
- Is there any specific, surprising detail, or only safe generalities?
- After cleaning, is it still voiceless?

## How to apply to a draft
Reply in the same language as the passage (for Spanish, also cut filler such as "cabe destacar," "en el mundo actual," "desempeña un papel fundamental," and "no solo X, sino también Y"). First give a short diagnosis listing the main tells found, with quoted examples. Then write a draft rewrite, ask yourself what still makes it obviously machine-written and answer in a few bullets, and finish with a final rewrite that fixes those, contains no em or en dashes, and keeps the original length of coverage and the author's voice. Return the diagnosis, the remaining-tells bullets, and the final rewrite; omit the draft unless asked.


## Deep notes
Before and after patterns to copy.
- Inflated significance. Before: "The institute was established in 1989, marking a pivotal moment in the evolution of regional statistics." After: "The institute was established in 1989 to publish regional statistics independently of the national office." Replace the claim of importance with the fact that makes it important.
- Notability padding. Before: "Her views have been cited in several major outlets and she has a large social media following." After: name one real instance: "In a 2024 interview she argued that regulation should focus on outcomes rather than methods."
- Participle tails. Before: "The palette of blue, green, and gold resonates with the region, symbolizing bluebonnets and the Gulf, reflecting a deep connection to the land." After: "The temple uses blue, green, and gold. The architect said they refer to local bluebonnets and the Gulf coast." Keep the attributed fact and drop the interpretive trailer.
- Promotional tone. Before: "Nestled in the breathtaking region of Gonder, the town stands as a vibrant community with a rich heritage." After: "The town is in the Gonder region and is known for its weekly market and an 18th-century church."
- Vague authority. Before: "Experts believe it plays a crucial role in the ecosystem." After: "It supports several endemic fish species, according to a 2019 survey."
- Formulaic challenges. Before: "Despite challenges, the town continues to thrive." After: "Traffic worsened after three IT parks opened in 2015; the council began a drainage project in 2022."
- Copula avoidance. Before: "The gallery serves as the exhibition space and boasts over 3,000 square feet." After: "The gallery is the exhibition space. It has four rooms totaling 3,000 square feet."
- Negative parallelism. Before: "It's not merely a song, it's a statement." After: say what the song does. Clipped tails such as "no guessing" become a real clause: "without forcing the user to guess."
- Forced triples. Before: "keynotes, panels, and networking opportunities" and "innovation, inspiration, and industry insights." After: "talks and panels, with time to talk between sessions." Use two or four items when that is the true count.
- Hyphen pairs. AI hyphenates compounds uniformly, even after the noun. Keep "a high-quality report" and write "the report is high quality."
- Fragmented headers. Delete a one-line paragraph under a heading that only restates the heading ("Speed matters.").
- Diff-anchored text. "This function was added to replace the old loop" becomes a description of what the thing does now. Version-scoped documents (changelogs) are the exception.
- Fake candor. "Is it worth it? Honestly? It depends." becomes "Whether it's worth it depends on how often you'll use it."

Gap-filling and cutoff talk. Two tells: a paragraph about not finding information, then invented filler to cover the gap ("maintains a low profile," "likely grew up in a middle-class household"). Fix by stating what is unknown in one sentence, or cut. Never replace a missing fact with a plausible guess.

The soulless trap. Text with no AI tells can still read as machine-made if every sentence has the same length and shape, nothing is opinionated, and uncertainty is never acknowledged. Fixes: vary the rhythm, let one real opinion or mixed feeling show, add a specific unusual detail, allow an aside or a self-correction. Applies to essays, posts, and personal writing. In reference, legal, or technical text, plain and neutral is the correct human voice.

Before, clean but flat: "The experiment produced interesting results. Some developers were impressed while others were skeptical. The implications remain unclear." After, with a pulse: "I genuinely don't know how to feel about this one. Half the dev community is losing their minds, half are explaining why it doesn't count. The truth is probably somewhere boring in the middle."

False positives to leave alone. Perfect grammar. Mixed formal and casual register. Formal vocabulary that is not on the stock list. One "however" or "moreover." Curly quotes by themselves, since editors auto-curl. One em dash. One short emphatic sentence. "Honestly" mid-sentence. Letter-style openings and sign-offs. Unsourced claims. Do not rewrite watched phrases inside quotations, titles, proper names, or examples where the phrase itself is the subject. Judge by clusters: a single dash means nothing, but dashes plus a triple plus "vibrant tapestry" plus a "Conclusion" section is a pattern.

Human signs to preserve. A specific, hard-to-fabricate detail (a real address, a weird quote, "the lawyer who used to work upstairs from my dentist"). Mixed feelings and unresolved tension. Dated slang or references. A first-person choice the writer can defend. Uneven sentence lengths. Genuine parenthetical self-corrections.

The three-pass loop. Draft the rewrite and read it aloud. Ask what still makes it obviously machine-written and answer in brief bullets: typical answers are that it is too compressed (a rewrite that drops texture instead of rewriting it), explains the point too cleanly, or ends on a flat generic closer. Then write the final version fixing those. A five-paragraph input gets a five-paragraph output.
