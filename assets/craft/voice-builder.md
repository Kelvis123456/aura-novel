# voice-builder
## When to use
Use to learn how an author writes from a few samples and turn it into a reusable voice profile, so new writing sounds like them. It captures what the voice does and what it avoids.

## Core rules
- Work from the samples supplied. Do not invent patterns that are not there, and never invent facts, experiences, or metrics.
- Require at least 3 samples (3 to 5 is the target) before finding patterns. If fewer are given, ask for more. Look for patterns across all samples, not quirks from one piece.
- Extract voice signals: average sentence length; paragraph rhythm (single-line breaks, blank lines, staccato versus flowing); hook or opening style (contrarian, question, data point, story, confession, observation); point of view (first, second, observational); tone (deadpan, warm, blunt, playful, clinical); signature phrases and recurring words; closing style.
- Extract structural signals: length range, lists versus prose, how pieces open and close, how transitions are handled.
- Extract topic signals: subjects recurring across samples, the apparent audience, what the author stands for.
- Extract absence signals: words and punctuation consistently missing (for example em dashes in 0 of 5 samples), hook types never used, tones never hit, structures avoided.
- Write one integrated profile, with positive and absence signals together, under about 500 words. Sections: who I sound like (2-3 sentences), tone (3-5 attributes plus 1-2 tones never hit), sentence rhythm (including avoidance patterns such as no tricolons or no sentences over 25 words), hook patterns (3-5 with one example each), how I open, how I close, signature phrases, off-limits, and what this voice never does (3-5 specific behaviors, such as never using "not X, but Y").
- Fill every section from actual evidence. No generic filler. If a pattern is not present, say so.
- Separate explicit author prohibitions from patterns merely absent in a small sample. Label the latter provisional, with counts (for example "0 of 5 samples"). Absence alone does not establish a permanent ban.
- If samples contradict each other, note the contradiction instead of smoothing it over.
- Borrowed or starter samples describe a borrowed style, never the author's own experiences or established voice.
- Keep an author-facing brief of who they are: role, audience, 3-5 topic pillars, point of view, the one thing they want readers to think, and what they refuse to write about. Keep it under 300 words and do not duplicate it inside the voice profile.
- When drafting from a profile, follow it as written: rhythm, openings, closings, signature phrases, and every off-limits item. Use British English unless the samples are clearly American. Use no em dashes in any output.

## Diagnose
- Is the sample set big and varied enough, at least three pieces?
- What is the typical sentence length, and how does it vary?
- How do the pieces open and close?
- What tone is consistent, and what tone never appears?
- Which words, phrases, or punctuation recur, and which are always missing?
- Does the voice use lists, fragments, questions, or rhetorical devices, and how often?
- Which absences are deliberate bans, and which are just chance in a small sample?
- Do the samples contradict each other?
- Does a draft stray from these patterns anywhere?

## How to apply to a draft
Reply in the same language as the passage. If the user supplies several samples, return a voice profile with the sections listed above, each backed by a quoted example or a count. If the user supplies a single passage plus a profile (or one sample to match), first diagnose where the passage matches or departs from the voice (sentence length, rhythm, hooks, tone, signature phrases, absences), quoting the lines, then give a rewritten passage that fits the voice without changing its content. If fewer than three samples exist and no profile is given, say what you can see so far, label it provisional, and ask for more samples.
