# story-analysis
## When to use
Use after a short story or novel chapter is drafted, to evaluate its strengths, weaknesses and improvement opportunities against its own goals.

## Core rules
- Evaluate against what the story is trying to be, not against bestsellers. Identify genre expectations first; the same trait is a flaw in one genre and a feature in another.
- Diagnose structure before prose. Do not fixate on sentence-level issues while the macro structure fails.
- Prioritize by impact: report the 3-5 highest-impact issues, note what works, and make guidance actionable. A checklist surfaces possibilities, not mandates; a missing shattering moment is critical, a slight POV wobble may be a choice.
- Short story, Narrative Foundation: one clear premise or conflict statable in one sentence, scope fitting the length, premise integrated with character stakes, implications explored.
- Short story, Character: one or two main characters with clear stakes, characterization through action and choice not description, an arc that intersects the premise, supporting characters that each serve a purpose.
- Short story, Environment: details woven into action, each element serving multiple purposes (setting, plot, theme), clear and consistent rules, environment that affects choices. Flag info-dumps and details that never matter.
- Shattering Moment: the pivotal, irreversible point that transforms everything, through personal revelation, external event, relationship transformation, internal realization or cultural collision. In science fiction, technology is, enables or prevents recovery from it, or the moment changes the character's relationship with technology.
- Short story, Scene Structure: every scene advances character and plot, the opening hook establishes character, situation and conflict, the conflict is focused, the resolution satisfies while resonating beyond.
- Short story, Technical Execution: economy of detail, consistent and purposeful POV with minimal shifts, consistent tone, purposeful dialogue with subtext, style serving the story.
- Short story, Emotional Architecture: a clear emotional throughline, strategic build and release, earned payoff, something unspoken but felt.
- Short story, Thematic Resolution: the arc completes, premise implications are addressed, the immediate conflict resolves, larger questions linger.
- Chapter, role: define the chapter's job in the novel and its plot, character and theme contribution. Momentum: the opening connects to the previous chapter, the ending creates forward pull, questions answered versus raised stay balanced. Flag chapters that end at a natural stopping point or answer everything.
- Chapter, threading: track main plot and each subplot as advanced, set up or resolved. Check character continuity, POV and voice consistency, information flow and reader-versus-character knowledge, pacing relative to neighboring chapters, and connections to the previous chapter, next chapter and wider novel.

## Diagnose
- Can the core conflict be stated in one sentence?
- Is the scope right for the length?
- Is character shown through action, and does the protagonist change?
- Is there a clear, irreversible turning point?
- Does every scene advance character and plot?
- Is POV and tone consistent?
- Is the emotional payoff earned?
- Does the resolution satisfy while still resonating?
- For a chapter: what is its job, and does the ending pull the reader on?
- Which problems are structural, and which are only preference?

## How to apply to a draft
Reply in the same language as the passage. First give the analysis: state the genre and the story's apparent goal, list what works, then the 3-5 highest-impact weaknesses in priority order, each tied to a specific place in the text and labeled structural, character, emotional or technical. Use the short story criteria for a complete piece and the chapter criteria for a section of a longer work. Do not list every checklist miss. Then give a rewritten passage for the single most important problem (for example sharpening the opening hook, the shattering moment, or the chapter ending). Keep the author's voice, events and intent, and explain in one line what the rewrite changes.
