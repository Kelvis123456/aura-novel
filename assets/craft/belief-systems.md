# belief-systems
## When to use
Use when creating pantheons, religions, spiritual practices or religious institutions that must shape society and drive character motivation, or when an existing faith feels like shallow flavor.

## Core rules
- Build from the ten principles: experiential foundation (belief starts as an attempt to explain and influence significant experiences), ecological integration, social cohesion, institutional evolution (organizations grow more complex over time), power legitimation, narrative embedding (stories encode core values), ritual entrainment, adaptive reinterpretation, orthopraxy-orthodoxy balance (correct practice versus correct belief), and syncretism-boundedness tension (absorbing outside elements while keeping boundaries).
- Choose a position on each of ten parameter categories. Cosmology: creation narrative, cosmic structure, temporal framework (linear, cyclical, eternal, branching), natural order, eschatology. Divine/spirit: population (mono-, poly-, animism, pantheism, nontheism), character, divine-human relationship, accessibility, spirit ecology.
- Afterlife/destiny: geography, judgment mechanism, reincarnation, ancestral relations, end states. Ritual: frequency, participants, formalization, materials, emotional tone (ecstatic, solemn, celebratory, penitential, contemplative).
- Organization: leadership structure, specialist roles, initiation, institutional complexity, economic base. Ethics: moral foundation emphasis (care, fairness, loyalty, authority, purity, liberty), prohibitions, virtues, justice conception, outsider treatment.
- Epistemology: knowledge sources, interpretive authority, mystery versus clarity, doubt management, truth claims. Social integration: life-cycle rituals, identity markers, endogamy, communal obligations, boundary maintenance.
- Adaptation: reform mechanism, schism pattern, syncretism, crisis response, modernization. Experience: mystical techniques, altered states, miracle or magic systems, healing, possession.
- Pick one type on each axis: organizational (communal/tribal, ecclesiastical, congregational, teacher-disciple, individual), cosmological (monotheistic, polytheistic, henotheistic, animistic, pantheistic, nontheistic), soteriological (salvation, liberation, harmony, ancestral continuity, prosperity, enlightenment), social function (world-rejecting, world-transforming, world-affirming, identity-preserving, power-legitimating).
- Work in order: cosmology, doctrine (including an explanation for suffering and evil), ritual practice, institutional structure, social integration, historical development (founding, schisms, reformations), and material culture (sacred architecture, art, music, dress, symbols).
- Fantasy adaptation: verifiable divine presence, magic with concrete effects, multi-species theology, provable afterlife. Science fiction: machine-consciousness cults, xenotheology, virtual afterlife faiths. Post-apocalyptic: artifact veneration, catastrophe theodicy, remnant preservationists.
- Minimum deliverables: a founding narrative, 3-5 key rituals, a leadership structure, at least one schism or variation, distinctive identity markers, and a historical arc.
- Avoid Monolithic Belief (give at least two factions, plus skeptics, reformers and zealots), Theology Without Practice (design daily observances and material culture), Simplistic Good/Evil Dualism (opponents have reasons and legitimate grievances), Functional Deity (gods that are only magic power sources; ask what the deity means and what would be lost without it), and Present Values in Ancient Costume (let beliefs shape values, including uncomfortable ones).
- Integrate: sacred language and liturgy, church-state relations and religious law, religious property and charity, status and marriage rules.

## Diagnose
- What experience or fear does this faith exist to explain or control?
- What do believers do daily, weekly and at birth, marriage and death?
- Where do believers disagree, and who are the reformers, traditionalists and doubters?
- Is the deity a vending machine for powers, or an object of meaning?
- Who gains authority from this belief, and who pays for the clergy?
- Do characters hold values that the stated doctrine would actually forbid or never produce?
- Is the environment visible in the practices, calendar and symbols?
- How does it treat outsiders and handle doubt?
- Has it changed over time, or does it read as eternal?

## How to apply
Reply in the language of the request. Return a paste-ready faith sheet with these headings: Overview (type on each of the four axes); Cosmology and Doctrine (creation, structure, time, afterlife, explanation of suffering); Divine/Spirit (who, how they relate to humans); Practice (3-5 rituals with frequency, participants, tone, plus life-cycle rites and calendar); Institution (leadership, specialists, funding, relation to political power); Ethics and Boundaries (prohibitions, virtues, outsiders, doubt); History (founding story, at least one schism, reforms); Material Culture (architecture, dress, symbols, sacred language terms); Internal Disagreements (two or more factions, with each side's argument); Story Hooks (how the belief pressures character choices). Mark invented content as proposals and keep it consistent with the user's existing lore.


## Deep notes

Parameter options to pick from, so choices are specific rather than generic:
- Creation: big-bang allegory, divine crafting, emergence from chaos, eternal universe. Cosmic structure: layered heavens and hells, parallel planes, one unified cosmos, dream-reality duality. Natural order: divine law, impersonal forces, conscious universe, random chance. Eschatology: final judgment, eternal return, heat death, transformation.
- Divine character: benevolent, indifferent, capricious, evolving, unknowable. Divine-human relationship: parental, contractual, adversarial, merged, distant. Access: always available, ritual-gated, unpredictable, mediated. Spirit ecology: hierarchical, territorial, functional, competitive, cooperative.
- Afterlife: heaven/hell binary, multiple realms, reabsorption, persistence. Judgment: moral weighing, faith-based, lineage-based, arbitrary. Reincarnation: merit-based, random, purpose-driven, choice-based. Ancestors: active intercession, memory only, merged with the living, forgotten. End states: eternal bliss, dissolution, transformation, continuation.
- Ritual formalization runs from strict prescription through guided improvisation to free expression; materials run from blood sacrifice and offerings through symbolic objects to nothing material.
- Leadership: hierarchical, collegial, charismatic, democratic, leaderless. Specialists: priests, shamans, prophets, scholars, healers, or none. Initiation: by birth, achievement, revelation or choice. Economic base: donations, taxes, production, services, state support.
- Justice: retributive, restorative, karmic, divine. Outsider treatment: missionary, exclusive, tolerant, hostile. Prohibitions: dietary, sexual, economic, social, temporal.
- Knowledge sources: revelation, tradition, reason, experience, authority. Mystery handling: esoteric secrets, open knowledge, or layered access by rank. Doubt: forbidden, encouraged, tolerated, or required. Truth claims: exclusive, inclusive, relative, pragmatic.
- Identity markers: clothing, diet, calendar, language, location. Endogamy ranges from strict in-group marriage to open. Boundary maintenance: strict, permeable, invisible, contested.
- Reform mechanism: prophet-led, scholarly, democratic, top-down. Schism pattern: doctrinal, political, regional, generational. Crisis response: renewal, retrenchment, splintering, dissolution. Modernization: embracing, resistant, selective, parallel.
- Mystical techniques: meditation, prayer, asceticism, service. Altered states: trance, substances, dance, breath work. Healing: spiritual, faith-based, integrated with ordinary medicine, or separate. Possession: accepted, feared, sought, denied.

Type details worth using. Communal/tribal faith is fused with kinship, so leaving the faith means leaving the family. Ecclesiastical faith is hierarchical and bureaucratic. Congregational faith has semi-autonomous local communities. Teacher-disciple faith passes knowledge down lineages. Henotheism keeps one supreme deity among many. Salvation religions rescue people from a bad spiritual state; liberation systems free them from suffering or illusion; harmony frameworks align people with cosmic order; prosperity orientations promise material success as divine favor. World-rejecting faiths withdraw ascetically; world-transforming faiths push social change; world-affirming faiths engage society; identity-preserving faiths guard cultural continuity.

Setting-specific faiths to borrow from:
- Fantasy: demonstrable divine intervention (then doubt becomes the interesting question, and faith shifts from "does the god exist" to "is the god worthy"); spiritual practice with concrete magical effects; theology that must address several sentient races; a provable afterlife; city or architecture shaped by divine will.
- Science fiction: religious response to AI; faiths of guided evolutionary transcendence; human reaction to alien life; practices adapted to space habitation; digital-afterlife faiths built on consciousness preservation.
- Post-apocalyptic: worship of pre-collapse technology; theodicy for the catastrophe; survival faiths that sacralize practical knowledge; groups preserving pre-collapse traditions as remnants.

Worked build, in the recommended order. A salt-marsh people who lose a third of their children to fever might make the dead return to the tide (cosmology), teach that fever is a debt the living owe the water (explanation for suffering), keep a daily salt-line at the doorstep and a seasonal drowning-feast (practice), and let tide-wardens collect a share of every catch (institution and funding). A reform wing then argues the debt can be paid in labor on drainage works, and the traditionalists call that bargaining with the water. That single quarrel gives you a schism, a political conflict and character pressure at once.

Step checklists to run per layer:
- Doctrine: core concepts, a sacred narrative framework, key ethical principles, a spiritual path or progression, and an account of suffering, evil and misfortune.
- Ritual: regular worship patterns, life-cycle ceremonies, a calendar of observances, sacred-site activities, and a method for mystical experience.
- Institution: leadership, specialist roles and their training, hierarchy, economic support, and the relation to political authority.
- Social integration: membership boundaries and requirements, which identity markers are distinct and which are shared with neighbors, insider and outsider treatment, family and kinship rules, community support and obligation.
- History: founding and early development, schisms and reformations, adaptation to cultural change, relations with other religions, regional and demographic variants.
- Material culture: sacred architecture and spaces, art, music and literature, clothing and adornment, sacred objects and symbols.

Failure modes beyond the named ones: a single function (religion that only comforts, or only legitimates the king) when real faiths touch diet, calendar, marriage, law and money at once; no adaptation to environment; and a faith that never argues with itself. Whenever you finish a sheet, check that at least two factions would answer the same crisis differently.
