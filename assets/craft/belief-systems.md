# belief-systems
## When to use
Use when creating pantheons, religions, spiritual practices or religious institutions that must shape society and drive character motivation, or when an existing faith feels like shallow flavor.

## Core rules
- Build from the ten principles: experiential foundation (belief starts as an attempt to explain and influence significant experiences), ecological integration, social cohesion, institutional evolution (organizations grow more complex over time), power legitimation, narrative embedding (stories encode core values), ritual entrainment, adaptive reinterpretation, orthopraxy-orthodoxy balance (correct practice versus correct belief), and syncretism-boundedness tension (absorbing outside elements while keeping boundaries).
- Choose a position on each of ten parameter categories. Cosmology: creation narrative, cosmic structure, temporal framework (linear, cyclical, eternal, branching), natural order, eschatology. Divine/spirit: population (mono-, poly-, animism, pantheism, nontheism), character, divine-human relationship, accessibility, spirit ecology.
- Afterlife/destiny: geography, judgment mechanism, reincarnation, ancestral relations, end states. Ritual: frequency, participants, formalization, materials, emotional tone (ecstatic, solemn, celebratory, penitential, contemplative).
- Organization: leadership structure, specialist roles, initiation, institutional complexity, economic base. Ethics: moral foundation emphasis (care, fairness, loyalty, authority, purity, liberty), prohibitions, virtues, justice conception, outsider treatment.
- Epistemology: knowledge sources, interpretive authority, mystery versus clarity, doubt management, truth claims. Social integration: life-cycle rituals, identity markers, endogamy, communal obligations, boundary maintenance.
- Adaptation: reform mechanism, schism pattern, syncretism, crisis response, modernization. Experience: mystical techniques, altered states, miracle or magic systems, healing, possession.
- Pick one type on each axis: organizational (communal/tribal, ecclesiastical, congregational, teacher-disciple, individual), cosmological (monotheistic, polytheistic, henotheistic, animistic, pantheistic, nontheistic), soteriological (salvation, liberation, harmony, ancestral continuity, prosperity, enlightenment), social function (world-rejecting, world-transforming, world-affirming, identity-preserving, power-legitimating).
- Work in order: cosmology, doctrine (including an explanation for suffering and evil), ritual practice, institutional structure, social integration, historical development (founding, schisms, reformations), and material culture (sacred architecture, art, music, dress, symbols).
- Fantasy adaptation: verifiable divine presence, magic with concrete effects, multi-species theology, provable afterlife. Science fiction: machine-consciousness cults, xenotheology, virtual afterlife faiths. Post-apocalyptic: artifact veneration, catastrophe theodicy, remnant preservationists.
- Minimum deliverables: a founding narrative, 3-5 key rituals, a leadership structure, at least one schism or variation, distinctive identity markers, and a historical arc.
- Avoid Monolithic Belief (give at least two factions, plus skeptics, reformers and zealots), Theology Without Practice (design daily observances and material culture), Simplistic Good/Evil Dualism (opponents have reasons and legitimate grievances), Functional Deity (gods that are only magic power sources; ask what the deity means and what would be lost without it), and Present Values in Ancient Costume (let beliefs shape values, including uncomfortable ones).
- Integrate: sacred language and liturgy, church-state relations and religious law, religious property and charity, status and marriage rules.

## Diagnose
- What experience or fear does this faith exist to explain or control?
- What do believers do daily, weekly and at birth, marriage and death?
- Where do believers disagree, and who are the reformers, traditionalists and doubters?
- Is the deity a vending machine for powers, or an object of meaning?
- Who gains authority from this belief, and who pays for the clergy?
- Do characters hold values that the stated doctrine would actually forbid or never produce?
- Is the environment visible in the practices, calendar and symbols?
- How does it treat outsiders and handle doubt?
- Has it changed over time, or does it read as eternal?

## How to apply
Reply in the language of the request. Return a paste-ready faith sheet with these headings: Overview (type on each of the four axes); Cosmology and Doctrine (creation, structure, time, afterlife, explanation of suffering); Divine/Spirit (who, how they relate to humans); Practice (3-5 rituals with frequency, participants, tone, plus life-cycle rites and calendar); Institution (leadership, specialists, funding, relation to political power); Ethics and Boundaries (prohibitions, virtues, outsiders, doubt); History (founding story, at least one schism, reforms); Material Culture (architecture, dress, symbols, sacred language terms); Internal Disagreements (two or more factions, with each side's argument); Story Hooks (how the belief pressures character choices). Mark invented content as proposals and keep it consistent with the user's existing lore.
