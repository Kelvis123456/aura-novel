# margin-notes
## When to use
Use when the author wants an editor's read of a passage without a rewrite: marginal comments anchored to exact lines, the way a human editor annotates a manuscript.

## Core rules
- Do not rewrite the passage. Annotate it. The author keeps their text and decides.
- Anchor every note to an exact quote of 3 to 12 words copied verbatim from the passage, so the author can find it with a search.
- One problem or one observation per note. Say what the reader experiences at that point, then what is causing it, then a direction to try. Never "improve this" with nothing else.
- Cover the things a line editor catches: sentences that tell what the scene already shows, filter words, repeated words or images within a few paragraphs, weak or stacked adverbs, dialogue that explains itself, a mismatch of point of view or tense, a detail that contradicts an earlier one, a beat that stalls the pace, an unclear pronoun.
- Also mark what works. A note that names a strong line and why it works teaches the author what to keep. Aim for roughly one praise note for every three problems.
- Rank by weight. Mark each note as MAJOR (changes how the scene reads), MINOR (a line-level fix) or KEEP (praise). Report MAJOR first.
- Never moralize about content. Judge craft only: does it do what the author is clearly trying to do.
- Give between 6 and 15 notes for a chapter, fewer for a short passage. Do not pad.

## Diagnose
- Where does attention drift, and which sentence is responsible?
- Which words or images repeat within a page?
- Where does a character explain what the reader already understands?
- Which line is the strongest, and is the scene built toward it?
- Does any detail contradict the established facts?

## How to apply to a draft
Reply in the same language as the passage. Output only a numbered list. Each item has this shape: the quoted words in guillemets, then the label in brackets, then the note. Example: 3. «sintió un escalofrío» [MINOR] Filter word plus a stock reaction. Name the cold itself, where it lands on the body, and let the reader draw the fear. Finish with one line starting with "Overall:" that says the single change that would lift the passage most.
