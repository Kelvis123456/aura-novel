# Long-Form Genres

## When to use
Use when choosing or positioning a genre, planning a serial's core hook and structure, matching reader expectations for a genre, or writing a scene or outline "in this genre".

## Core rules
- Build in this order: core selling point, then emotional pattern, then concrete plot. Fix the emotion the reader opens the book to feel before choosing events.
- Define the core hook in three layers: theme, genre core (what that genre uniquely offers), core emotion (a full chain from expectation to satisfaction). Leaving the genre core abandons the main reason readers came.
- Every chapter must hold an expectation point, a payoff point, or a bridge between them. A chapter with neither feels soulless and loses readers.
- Keep one main emotion running through the book; fold small emotions under it. Scattered emotion is the first cause of collapse.
- Limit innovation to three micro-innovations in character, relationships or plot, inside the template's boundary. Five methods: refine, upgrade, add an ingredient, invert, combine.
- Run at least three layers of conflict: vertical (superior and subordinate), horizontal (peers, rivals), and crossed (A against B, B against C, so A against C).
- Keep a core cycle of goal, action, result, gain, new goal. Fight fatigue by shifting from number contests to mechanism contests.
- Raise payoff thresholds: each payoff beats the last on reach, rank of the people involved, size of the gain or depth of the reversal.
- Give the power-up device one simple function the reader grasps at once. Too strong with too little conflict is boring; too weak against too much conflict makes readers anxious; ideal is a device that solves today's problem and exposes a bigger one.
- Match the device to the genre's pressure level and to the protagonist's life or job; add no new device often.
- Keep two or more expectation lines alive at all times. Just before the hero gets what he wants, hang another hook on it.
- Check the selling point every ten chapters: is what drew readers still here? Replacing it quietly drives readers away.
- Keep tone constant. A change of tone makes a different book. A blended genre keeps one genre as the main tone and uses the other as an outer shell or device.
- Use a clear low-status opening: one or two sources of pressure, information carried by dialogue, the device arrives only after the hero actively tries and falls short, and the first change is visible at once.
- Pass the opening five tests: simple (who, where, what he has, why, what he must do), on the main line, fast, a payoff early, never flat.
- Calibrate to the platform's readers: short and fast with strong emotion for download-heavy audiences; slower, steadier for long-line readers; persona and play for fandom-oriented ones. Use samples from the target platform, not memory.
- For female-audience long stories, give safety first (the heroine will win), keep the heroine active, put her emotional upgrades on her career or growth beats, and ration suffering so that each painful stretch ends in a reversal or sweetness.
- Imitate structure, drama techniques, character types; never imitate wording, signature scenes or style.
- Run romance on career: in male-oriented lines the hero does not chase; the love interest comes through his achievements. Female-oriented lines deepen connection.
- Plan the ending first, then build the late-book payoffs around it.

## Diagnose
- Can I say in one sentence what emotion the reader opens this book for?
- Does every chapter carry an expectation, a payoff, or a bridge?
- Is the genre's own core still delivered, or has the book drifted?
- Does the power-up device solve problems and reveal bigger ones?
- Are conflicts running on at least three layers?
- Are payoffs rising, or repeating at the same size?
- Do title, blurb, opening and content promise the same thing?
- Is the pacing right for the target platform?
- Are there at most three micro-innovations and two to three core hooks?

## How to apply
When asked to write a scene or outline in a genre, answer in the language of the request. State the genre, the core emotion and the beat from the genre's formula the scene occupies. Deliver the genre's required scenes and rules, then the payoff sized to the setup. For positioning requests, return core hook in the three layers, three micro-innovations, the conflict network, and a blurb that promises a win. Do not name real books or invent market data; say the heat of a genre needs validation.

## Deep notes
Pressure and payoff mechanics. Payoff formula: produce a want, give hope, strive, get the wish. Revenge fiction: first make the reader unhappy, then remove the unhappiness; anger resolves into relief. Cycle a cliche five ways: straight, inverted, inverted again, repeatedly inverted, inverted and then straight. Reader tiers: wants (power, comfort, safety, respect, self-realization); a story crossing tiers has hit potential. Deep need behind the surface: to be recognized, understood, respected.

Genre beat structures (percent of the story).
Chasing the wife to the crematorium: hook with her pain 5, abuse deepens 10, awakening 10, leaving and rebirth 15, his regret 10, career rise 15, repeated pleading and refusals 20, ending 15. Her leaving without looking back is the core.
Rebirth revenge: revelation 5, rebirth and aims 5, past and present interleaved 15, layer-by-layer revenge with new problems 25, counterattack and trust crisis 15, decisive blow 15, villains' fates 10, new life 10. Villains nest small, mid, final.
Betrayal in marriage: daily pressure 10, discovery 15, decision to leave 10, rebuilding career 20, the ex pesters 10, counterstrike 15, retribution 10, new life 10. The wife has a plan, never naive.
Everyday injustice (small-town realism): the oddball appears 10, oppression grows 15, pressure peaks 10, first counterstrike 15, backlash 10, face-slap 15, retribution 15, kindness repaid 10. Detail beats events.
Xianxia and fantasy: blow lands 5, conflict deepens 10, first counterstrike 15, greatest crisis 15, breakthrough 10, decisive strike 15, reckoning 15, new journey 15. Keep the power system clear, strategy over raw numbers, an uneven but steady rise.
Death-of-the-heroine drama: abuse 20, her death 5, regret 10, truth 20, mastermind 10, retribution 15, closure 10, aftermath 10. Core feeling: too late.
Urban martial: money drives; layered maps (school, dojo, bureau, university, military, interstellar); bridge maps with linking characters.
Sweet romance: meeting, pursuit, confirmation, obstacle, resolution; sweetness density sets stickiness; obstacles neither too strong nor too weak; the heroine needs her own shine.
Mortal-cultivator: cautious hero, weighing gains and losses; caution must be real, not just claimed; slow, finely foreshadowed.
Alternate history: the modern mind is the great device; spread shock by region and class; players want thrill over fidelity.
Entertainment industry: talent display and onlooker reaction chain; invented stars avoid real-person issues.
Other: rule-based horror (rules, route, dead end), long-life (the ordinary era is best), Western fantasy (start low), new media (emotion first, believable logic), comedy (logic inside the rules, plus fresh use of the joke's inner logic), infinite loop (20 to 30 chapters per dungeon plus a main line), suspense (mood, paced release), regret fiction (avoid the broken-engagement shell).

Short-form formulas and genre notes. Public showdown: set a public arena, let the villain win first, the hero lifts a glass or straightens a jacket calmly, evidence emerges one piece at a time, the villain breaks (smug, flustered, hopeless, collapsed), a signature line, the hero leaves without looking back. Revenge pace: a small slap every three to five beats, backlash within two or three beats of insult. Hook density: revenge every two beats; suspense every two to three; emotional every three; period and family every three to four. Female revenge relies on information gaps and truth exposure; male on power and identity, with the change of address revealing status.

Commercial notes. Title is the first gate, blurb the second, opening the third. Blurb types: four-part success (situation, conflict, action, success); three-part action; two-part suspense; four-part failure (revenge tales); three-part success (sweet). Combine success plus suspense for safety plus curiosity. Titles: identity contrast, reversal, plot-plus-payoff. Opening hunger: plant a question in the blurb, a small one in each chapter's opening, and withhold key information at the end. Open with one aim: survive. In 300 to 500 words state the situation, the danger and the hope.

Female-audience long stories. The four-part blurb must show the heroine's success or a way to win; a blurb that is all suffering scares readers off. Volume-level romance rhythm: flirtation and tension, confirmation at a plot climax, crisis (never suffering for its own sake), then only earned sweetness. Revenge or palace-intrigue plots tier their enemies from minor to mid to final and clear them volume by volume. Two or three core hooks at most; career needs a romance alongside it. Platform positioning: strong hooks and quick feedback suit short chapters; fine characters and slow burn suit longer, steadier serials.

Failure modes: one-shot gimmick stretched past its life; a mystery that never answers (riddle-speaking instead of foreshadowing); all characters turned into tools; the world emptied of old characters when the map changes; clashing genres with two alternating rhythms; pure romance with no career.
