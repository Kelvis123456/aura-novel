# governance-systems
## When to use
Use when designing kingdoms, empires, federations or any political entity that needs believable internal complexity and external relationships, rather than a monolithic evil empire or planet of hats.

## Core rules
- Build from eight principles: evolutionary development, environmental adaptation, cultural stratification, power diffusion realism (authority is rarely absolute and is contested along several dimensions), conflict-cooperation balance, institutional hybridity (no pure models), boundary fluidity, and fractal organization (similar patterns repeat at different scales).
- Choose values across ten parameter categories. Geography: terrain and boundaries, resources and claims, communication barriers, environmental threats, spatial scale. History: formation pathway (conquest, colonization, migration, revolution), age patterns, external influence, critical junctures, institutional inheritance.
- Demography: density, ethnic or species mix, migration, urbanization, age structure. Economy: resource economy, disparity, trade dependency, technology, infrastructure. Culture: linguistic diversity, belief distribution, cohesion forces, divergence forces, competing historical narratives.
- Power: authority distribution, legitimization, elite composition, opposition organization, external relationships. Institutions: governance mechanism, legal tradition, military organization, civic engagement, bureaucratic capacity. Technology: communication, transport, military, surveillance, extraction.
- Temporal: cycle stage (formation, expansion, stability, contraction, transformation), crisis frequency, adaptation rate, generational politics, historical consciousness. External: diplomatic position, threat environment, cultural exchange, economic integration, rank in regional hierarchies.
- Select a polity type. Nation-state forms: unitary state, federal system, confederacy, constitutional monarchy, absolute monarchy, military dictatorship, single-party state, theocracy, city-state, tribal confederation. Non-state: corporate authority, religious order domain, criminal syndicate territory, autonomous zone, mercenary guild, nomadic federation, trade league, technocratic enclave. Interstellar: hegemonic empire, democratic federation, interspecies concordat, colonial protectorate, trading collective, security alliance, stellar aristocracy.
- Set internal cohesion: high unity, factional, regional division, class stratification, or fragmentation.
- Set territorial control: effective, negotiated, layered authority, contested zones, or nominal claim. Distant provinces get more autonomy.
- Name the legitimacy source: traditional, charismatic, legal-rational, performance, or ideological. Name the power-transition pattern: orderly succession, elite selection, popular contest, violent overthrow, or external imposition.
- Scale administrative reach to communication and transport technology. Central directives are general principles, not detailed commands, and local variation is the norm.
- Name the polity to match its era: foundation (descriptive, founder-based), expansion (grandiose), consolidation (institutional), transformation (rebranded), decline (past-glorifying, territorial overstatement).
- Work in order: environment, history, population, economic network, political structure, relationship network, layered identities (cross-cutting loyalties), dynamic elements (reformers, opposition, succession disputes).
- Avoid the Evil Empire (explain what citizens get from the bargain and who defends it), Planet of Hats (at least three factions per polity plus generational, regional and class differences), Administrative Implausibility, Static Political Stasis (include a recent crisis and transformations), and Binary Conflict (at least three major powers, neutrals, mercenaries, opportunists, factions that could flip).

## Diagnose
- Does the ruler's reach exceed what messengers, roads and arms can deliver?
- Why do ordinary people and elites support this regime?
- Are there at least three internal factions, and what do they want from each other?
- What is the source of legitimacy, and what challenges it?
- How did this system evolve from earlier forms, and what crisis happened recently?
- Are borders drawn to resources, geography and strategic value?
- Are there neutral or opportunistic parties between the big blocs?
- Do identities cross-cut, so that loyalties pull in more than one direction?
- What is the succession question, and who stands to gain or lose?

## How to apply
Reply in the language of the request. Return a paste-ready polity sheet with these headings: Polity (name, type, naming logic matching its era); Environment and Origins (geography, resources, formation story, critical junctures); Population (composition, tensions); Power Structure (authority distribution, legitimacy, elites, opposition); Institutions (decision-making, law, military, bureaucracy, civic role); Control Map (cohesion level, territorial control by region); Factions (three or more, each with goals and base); External Relations (allies, rivals, dependencies, neutrals); Identity Layers; Dynamics (cycle stage, current crisis, reform movement, succession issue); Story Hooks. Mark invented content as proposals, keep consistent with the user's lore, and note where administrative reach is limited by technology.


## Deep notes

Questions to answer for each parameter, so the entry is more than a label:
- Geography: how terrain and climate shape boundaries; how key resources drive territorial claims; what obstacles block cultural or political unification; what shared dangers push cooperation or rivalry; what distance means for governing.
- History: the origin context; how governance changed across periods; what outside powers left behind; which critical junctures shaped current structure; which older systems still influence it.
- Demography: how density and spread affect governance; the political meaning of ethnic or species mix; how migration shifts stability; the urban-rural balance; the political effects of age structure.
- Economy: extraction, manufacturing or information base; the political effect of wealth gaps; which external trade ties create influence; what tools of control the technology allows; what physical connections link territories.
- Culture: which language patterns create barriers; the geography of belief and ideology; what promotes shared identity and what promotes distinct identities; which competing readings of shared history exist.
- Power: how centralized decisions are; the background and nature of the elite; how resistance is organized; which alliances, dependencies and rivalries exist.
- Institutions: how decisions are made and carried out; what law rests on; who controls coercive forces; how people take part; how professional the administration is.
- Temporal: stage of the cycle; the pattern of existential crises; speed of adaptation; age-cohort differences in politics; whether the population looks to the past or the future.
- External: alliance, neutrality or isolation; the nature and closeness of threats; how values and information flow in; trade dependencies; rank in regional or global hierarchies.

Polity naming patterns. Unitary: [Place] Republic, Kingdom or State. Federal: United or Federated [Places or Peoples]. Confederacy: Confederation, League or Alliance of [X]. Constitutional monarchy: Kingdom, Emirate or Sultanate of [Place]. Absolute monarchy: Empire or Dominion of [Ruler or Dynasty]. Military dictatorship: [Place] Military Council or Junta. Single-party state: People's or Democratic Republic of [Place]. Theocracy: Holy State or Divine Realm. City-state: Free City of [Place]. Tribal confederation: [People] Nations or Tribes.
- Non-state: corporate authority ([Industry] Corporation of [Place]); religious order domain (Order or Brotherhood of [Faith Symbol]); criminal territory ([Family or Symbol] Syndicate); autonomous zone (Autonomous Region of [Place]); mercenary guild ([Founder or Symbol] Company or Legion); nomadic federation (Horde, Caravan or Fleet); trade league (Merchant Council or Exchange); technocratic enclave (Institute of [Place]).
- Interstellar: hegemonic empire (Imperium or Dominion with vassals); democratic federation (voluntary union of equals); interspecies concordat (treaty-based, Compact of [Founding Place]); colonial protectorate (external control claimed as benevolent); trading collective; security alliance (Defense Pact); stellar aristocracy (Houses or Dynasties ruling star systems).

Behavior levels in more detail. Cohesion: high unity means strong shared identity and willingness to sacrifice; factional means competing identity groups and coalition politics; regional division means local loyalty beats the center; class stratification means solidarity runs horizontally across vertical divisions; fragmentation means overlapping divisions and unstable alliances. Territorial control: effective means consistent enforcement and taxation; negotiated means the center compromises with local powers; layered means overlapping claims and situational compliance; contested means active competition for loyalty; nominal means ownership asserted with minimal control.

Legitimacy and its stress points:
- Traditional: reverence for established practice and hereditary right; breaks when a line fails or a custom is violated.
- Charismatic: personal loyalty to an exceptional leader; breaks at death and at the succession.
- Legal-rational: respect for procedure and institutions; breaks when procedure is seen as rigged.
- Performance: support in exchange for tangible benefits; breaks at the first bad harvest or lost war.
- Ideological: commitment to shared values; breaks through schism over interpretation.
Transitions: orderly succession gives predictable change and policy continuity; elite selection means limited competition inside the ruling class; popular contest brings mass participation and possible rapid swings; violent overthrow gives instability and discontinuity; external imposition has questionable legitimacy and invites resistance.

Naming over time: foundation names are descriptive, founder-based or geographic; expansion names are grandiose and inclusive; consolidation names are institutional and stability-minded; transformation names are rebranded and reform-signaling; decline names glorify the past and overstate territory.

Worked administrative check. If a message takes eleven days by road in good weather, a governor effectively decides most things alone for a month at a stretch. Give the center a few levers it can pull from afar (appointments, tax collection dates, a standing army detachment) and leave the rest to local lords, who bargain. That makes negotiated control the default for distant provinces and gives you built-in conflict over what the center may ask.

Step prompts per layer:
- Environment: terrain and resources, natural barriers and links, threats and opportunities, technological context.
- History: formation stories for each entity, critical conflicts, cultural diffusion and divergence, inherited systems.
- Identity layers: several identity levels per population, cross-cutting identities, movements and their goals, identity markers.
- Dynamics: entities in different cycle stages, emerging threats, reform and opposition, succession disputes.

Extra failure signs: simplistic allegory of a real state (build an unusual combination of features), cultural uniformity, and resource blindness (borders that ignore the strategic resources next to them).
