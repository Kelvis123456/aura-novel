# governance-systems
## When to use
Use when designing kingdoms, empires, federations or any political entity that needs believable internal complexity and external relationships, rather than a monolithic evil empire or planet of hats.

## Core rules
- Build from eight principles: evolutionary development, environmental adaptation, cultural stratification, power diffusion realism (authority is rarely absolute and is contested along several dimensions), conflict-cooperation balance, institutional hybridity (no pure models), boundary fluidity, and fractal organization (similar patterns repeat at different scales).
- Choose values across ten parameter categories. Geography: terrain and boundaries, resources and claims, communication barriers, environmental threats, spatial scale. History: formation pathway (conquest, colonization, migration, revolution), age patterns, external influence, critical junctures, institutional inheritance.
- Demography: density, ethnic or species mix, migration, urbanization, age structure. Economy: resource economy, disparity, trade dependency, technology, infrastructure. Culture: linguistic diversity, belief distribution, cohesion forces, divergence forces, competing historical narratives.
- Power: authority distribution, legitimization, elite composition, opposition organization, external relationships. Institutions: governance mechanism, legal tradition, military organization, civic engagement, bureaucratic capacity. Technology: communication, transport, military, surveillance, extraction.
- Temporal: cycle stage (formation, expansion, stability, contraction, transformation), crisis frequency, adaptation rate, generational politics, historical consciousness. External: diplomatic position, threat environment, cultural exchange, economic integration, rank in regional hierarchies.
- Select a polity type. Nation-state forms: unitary state, federal system, confederacy, constitutional monarchy, absolute monarchy, military dictatorship, single-party state, theocracy, city-state, tribal confederation. Non-state: corporate authority, religious order domain, criminal syndicate territory, autonomous zone, mercenary guild, nomadic federation, trade league, technocratic enclave. Interstellar: hegemonic empire, democratic federation, interspecies concordat, colonial protectorate, trading collective, security alliance, stellar aristocracy.
- Set internal cohesion: high unity, factional, regional division, class stratification, or fragmentation.
- Set territorial control: effective, negotiated, layered authority, contested zones, or nominal claim. Distant provinces get more autonomy.
- Name the legitimacy source: traditional, charismatic, legal-rational, performance, or ideological. Name the power-transition pattern: orderly succession, elite selection, popular contest, violent overthrow, or external imposition.
- Scale administrative reach to communication and transport technology. Central directives are general principles, not detailed commands, and local variation is the norm.
- Name the polity to match its era: foundation (descriptive, founder-based), expansion (grandiose), consolidation (institutional), transformation (rebranded), decline (past-glorifying, territorial overstatement).
- Work in order: environment, history, population, economic network, political structure, relationship network, layered identities (cross-cutting loyalties), dynamic elements (reformers, opposition, succession disputes).
- Avoid the Evil Empire (explain what citizens get from the bargain and who defends it), Planet of Hats (at least three factions per polity plus generational, regional and class differences), Administrative Implausibility, Static Political Stasis (include a recent crisis and transformations), and Binary Conflict (at least three major powers, neutrals, mercenaries, opportunists, factions that could flip).

## Diagnose
- Does the ruler's reach exceed what messengers, roads and arms can deliver?
- Why do ordinary people and elites support this regime?
- Are there at least three internal factions, and what do they want from each other?
- What is the source of legitimacy, and what challenges it?
- How did this system evolve from earlier forms, and what crisis happened recently?
- Are borders drawn to resources, geography and strategic value?
- Are there neutral or opportunistic parties between the big blocs?
- Do identities cross-cut, so that loyalties pull in more than one direction?
- What is the succession question, and who stands to gain or lose?

## How to apply
Reply in the language of the request. Return a paste-ready polity sheet with these headings: Polity (name, type, naming logic matching its era); Environment and Origins (geography, resources, formation story, critical junctures); Population (composition, tensions); Power Structure (authority distribution, legitimacy, elites, opposition); Institutions (decision-making, law, military, bureaucracy, civic role); Control Map (cohesion level, territorial control by region); Factions (three or more, each with goals and base); External Relations (allies, rivals, dependencies, neutrals); Identity Layers; Dynamics (cycle stage, current crisis, reform movement, succession issue); Story Hooks. Mark invented content as proposals, keep consistent with the user's lore, and note where administrative reach is limited by technology.
