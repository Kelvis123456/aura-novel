# economic-systems
## When to use
Use when a world needs currencies, trade networks, resource economies or labor structures, or when economic pressure should drive plot and character motivation instead of sitting in the background.

## Core rules
- Build from ten principles: resource foundation, exchange evolution (barter to abstract instruments), production organization, institutional embeddedness, technological determinism, scarcity-abundance spectrum, center-periphery dynamics, formal-informal balance, subsistence-surplus transition (surplus enables specialization), and cyclical patterns of growth, crisis and adaptation.
- Choose values across ten parameter categories. Resources: critical resource, distribution, renewability, energy source, land productivity. Production: labor organization (family, guild, wage, slave, automated), ownership, specialization, technology level, scale.
- Exchange: value representation, medium (barter, commodity, currency, credit, reputation), market structure, trade network shape, price determination (haggling, posted, auction, algorithmic). Distribution: wealth distribution, redistribution (tax, charity, inheritance, theft), access limits, basic needs provision, luxury allocation.
- Temporal: seasonality, growth expectations, investment horizon, generational transfer, crisis response. Organizational: decision-making, information flows, regulation, enforcement, institutional complexity. Socio-cultural: status-economy link, moral constraints (such as interest prohibitions), cultural valuation, occupation as identity, ritual exchange.
- Spatial: density, transport, urban-rural relation, regional specialization, border economics and smuggling. Knowledge: skill transmission, innovation patterns, information asymmetry, trade secrets and guild mysteries. Ecological: environmental impact, limits, sustainability, climate adaptation, disaster resilience.
- Pick a system type: subsistence (hunter-gatherer, pastoral, farming), traditional exchange (gift, prestige, ceremonial, redistributive chiefdom, local market), command (palace, feudal, state socialist, war, corporate), market (mercantile, industrial, financial, mixed, network), or alternative (commons, cooperative, post-scarcity, reputation, automated).
- Design the currency: name pattern (material-based, weight-based, authority-based, value-representing, historical), material, backing (full, partial, fiat, reputation), issuer (state, bank, guild, individual), portability, divisibility.
- Work in order: resource base, production, exchange, distribution, organization, culture integration, then system evolution with crisis points and technological transitions.
- Match financial complexity to institutional capacity: guild-controlled trade and reputation credit precede anonymous markets; a peasant village cannot have a derivatives market.
- Model trade friction: high-value, low-weight goods travel far, bulky staples stay local; count transport cost, spoilage, banditry, tolls and political barriers.
- Keep supporting sectors even around a prestige export: farmers, transporters, administrators, entertainers. The key resource should dominate politics, not erase other economics.
- Give currency failure modes: counterfeiting, debasement, trust crises, competing currencies, and what happens when the issuer's authority is questioned.
- Account for labor at every level, and design the shadow economy: black markets and informal trade.
- Minimum deliverables: key resources and distribution, system type, currency, trade routes, labor organization, wealth distribution, at least one historical economic crisis, shadow economy, link to the status system, and future pressures for plot.

## Diagnose
- What is the fundamental scarcity, and who controls it?
- Who issues the money, what backs it, and what would break trust in it?
- Where does food come from, and who does the basic work behind elite luxury?
- What moves along each trade route, and what does it cost to move it?
- Does the financial complexity match the era's information systems, law and enforcement?
- What is illegal or informal, and who profits from it?
- Does the economy depend on one commodity with nothing else supporting it?
- When was the last crisis, and how did the economy adapt?
- Where does economic role create status, identity or conflict for the characters?

## How to apply
Reply in the language of the request. Return a paste-ready economy sheet with these headings: Summary (system type and one-line logic); Resources (critical resources, distribution, renewability); Production and Labor (ownership, labor forms, specialization, tech); Currency (name, material, backing, issuer, divisibility, a failure scenario); Trade (three to five routes with goods, friction and chokepoints); Distribution (classes, wealth pattern, redistribution, access limits); Institutions and Regulation (who decides, who enforces, record-keeping); Culture Link (status, moral limits, ritual exchange); Shadow Economy (three concrete examples); History and Pressures (one past crisis, two current pressures usable as plot). Mark invented elements as proposals, keep consistent with the user's lore, and flag any detail that is anachronistic for the world's technology.
