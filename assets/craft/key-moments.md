# key-moments
## When to use
Use when plotting feels mechanical, when the emotional beats of a story need defining, or when building a story from vivid scenes rather than from a plot outline.

## Core rules
- Define the story by the emotional experiences it delivers, not by plot mechanics. Identify the key moments the genre requires, sequence them for impact, then build everything else to make those moments possible.
- Work backward from vivid scenes to what must exist (world, characters, events) to make them happen. Do not outline first and then insert emotion; retrofitted emotion feels mechanical.
- Seven principles: emotional experience primacy; systemic integration with the world; character function alignment; visual-experiential priority (conceive moments as vivid scenes first); flexible sequencing; consequence cascades; bridging efficiency.
- Pick a primary and a secondary elemental genre. Choose 3-5 essential moments from the primary genre and 2-3 supporting ones from the secondary. Secondary supports; it must not overshadow the primary.
- Wonder moments: Initial Encounter, Scale Revelation, Perspective Shift, Wonder Escalation, Transcendent Integration.
- Mystery moments: Question Inception, Pattern Recognition, False Resolution, Progressive Revelation, Solution Crystallization.
- Adventure moments: Threshold Crossing, Capability Test, Resource Depletion, Ultimate Challenge, Return Transformation.
- Horror moments: Wrongness Glimpse, Safety Violation, Threat Escalation, Failed Solution, Confrontation.
- Thriller moments: Stakes Establishment, Deadline Imposition, Near Miss, Option Elimination, Decision Under Duress.
- Relationship moments: Significant Connection, Intimacy Deepening, Value Conflict, Relationship Crisis, Reconciliation/Resolution.
- Drama moments: Internal Conflict Revelation, External Pressure Point, Failure Moment, Truth Confrontation, Character Evolution.
- Issue moments: Perspective Challenge, Stake Personalization, Complexity Recognition, Position Testing, Perspective Integration.
- Ensemble moments: Group Formation, Role Establishment, Group Fracture, Collective Challenge, Synergy Moment.
- Visualize each moment concretely with sensory detail, and define how it changes a character or the reader's understanding of the world.
- Assign character functions (who experiences, enables or opposes each moment) and make the protagonist experience the most significant ones.
- Bridge scenes must serve at least two purposes: move toward the next moment AND develop character, reveal world or explore theme. Compress or cut logistics-only bridges.
- Plant setups early so later moments pay off; foreshadowing raises impact.
- Limit key moments to 5-8 per story. Let some scenes be quiet so peaks feel taller (avoid moment inflation).
- Every moment must follow from established character and world. If a character must act against their nature to reach it, the causation is forced.

## Diagnose
- What emotional experience should the reader have here, and does this moment deliver it?
- Does the moment belong to the primary genre, or has a secondary genre taken over?
- Does it follow logically from who the character is and how the world works?
- Is it vivid and concrete, or only a plot event?
- Does the emotional sequence progress, or repeat the same feeling?
- Are the bridges doing double duty, or just moving characters around?
- Was the setup planted early enough?
- Is everything treated as climactic?

## How to apply to a draft
Reply in the same language as the passage. First give a short diagnosis: name the genre and the key moment type the passage is attempting, the emotional experience it should create, and where it falls short (mechanical, mismatched, inflated, forced, or logistics-only). Then give a rewritten passage that makes the moment vivid and sensory, builds it from character and situation, lets the emotion emerge instead of being announced, and, if the passage is a bridge, gives it a second function. Keep the author's characters, events and voice. If the user supplies only a premise, return a short list of 5-8 key moments with emotional experience, a one-line scene concept each, and the bridge needs between them.
