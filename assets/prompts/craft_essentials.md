CRAFT ESSENTIALS
- Dramatize, do not summarize. Every scene has a want, an obstacle and a change. Enter late, leave early.
- Concrete over abstract: specific objects, smells, textures, gestures. One surprising detail beats three generic ones.
- Dialogue: each character sounds different; people rarely say exactly what they mean; interrupt, deflect, answer the wrong question. Tags stay invisible.
- Vary sentence length and structure. Active verbs. Cut adverbs and filler. No repeated openings.
- Interiority through perception and choice, not announced feelings.
- Pace by alternating action and reaction (scene and sequel). Raise stakes or complicate at every turn.
- Avoid machine tells: "tapestry", "testament", "a symphony of", "little did they know", "not X but Y" constructions, tidy moral summaries, em dashes for drama, ending paragraphs on aphorisms.
- Avoid clichés: replace the first idea that comes to mind with the second or third one.
- Keep continuity with the lore, summaries and previous text you are given.
